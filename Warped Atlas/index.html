    WarpedAtlas: An ARPG Overhaul for Terraria

    This project acts as a culmination of my taste in games. I have hundreds of hours within Terraria and Path of Exile (PoE) respectively. These two games have captured my interest for probably longer than they should, and as such, I wanted to not only do something constructive with that passion but use it as a springboard into game creation—an aspect of programming I had long sworn off due to my fear that it might sour my desire to keep playing games.

    The Vision: Sandbox Meets ARPG

    For the uninitiated, Terraria is a 2-D sandbox in which you can explore, build, and discover—from creating homes to slaying progressively harder creatures. Path of Exile and Diablo 2 are Action RPGs, which in my eyes are sandboxes of a different flavour. You create a character of a specific class, exploring procedurally generated levels and slowly amassing a character equipped to take down the most eldritch of horrors.

    My favourite aspects of these games can be boiled down to two core philosophies: 1. Feeling tangible increases in power. 2. The ability to build or work towards increasing this power in a specific direction.

    Archetypes & Mechanics

    I wanted a sandbox where I could kill monsters upon monsters, explore, and create powerful characters of varying flavours. For the time being, I have mapped these to the base classes of Terraria:

    • Mage/Sorcerer: Specialised spell casting.
    • Summoner: Minion and pet orchestration.
    • Melee Warrior: Close-quarters combat. I have introduced Warcries and shouts, inspired by the "singer barb" archetype in Diablo 2, where you create a character that quite literally shouts their enemies to death.
    • Ranger: Gun and bow wielding. Taking inspiration from PoE, I have introduced Traps into the weapon pool—akin to a claymore or a bear trap, but much more sensitive and explosive.

    Technical Architecture: Terraria as an Engine

    For any players of Terraria that pick up my mod, they will be immediately greeted by the fact I have taken Terraria more as an engine than something designed to be played alongside other popular mods.

    I wanted to create a standalone experience that uses only a single dependency known as Subworlds. This enables instanced environments separated from the base game: * A Hubworld where the player starts and returns. * Instanced Dungeons a player can fight through. * Boss Arenas to fight the various gatekeepers that block progression from one story act to the next.

    The Core Gameplay Loop: Looting dungeon chests (which hold randomised but tiered loot), killing monsters, allocating passives within a custom skill tree, and fighting bosses.

    Roadmap & Narrative

    Terraria doesn't natively use a very robust story NPC system; they act as basic vendors and cycle through simple dialogue. My goal is to extend the base NPC system to create a story-driven narrative where I can orchestrate cutscenes and tell a story.

    The current system uses custom UI, but seeks to avoid heavy visual overhauls until all required mechanics are fully fleshed out.
